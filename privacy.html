Privacy policy

Last updated October 23, 2026

The short version
  • There are no accounts, no sign-ups, no ads and no cookies.
  • Your progress is saved only in your own browser. It is never sent to me.
  • I count page views anonymously, so I can see which pages people use. I never see who you are.
  • I don't sell, rent or share data with anyone.

This policy covers AP Labs (jiacode2030.github.io/ap-labs/) and its course sites: Mechanics Lab, E&M Lab and Chemistry Lab, all under jiacode2030.github.io. They are free study sites made by JL, a student.

1. What stays on your device

The sites save your study progress in your browser's local storage, so it is still there next time. This data stays on your device. It is never sent to me or anyone else.

To delete it, clear this site's data in your browser settings. Using a private window means nothing is kept after you close it.

If you install the AP Labs app, your device also keeps a copy of the pages so they open offline. This copy is just the sites themselves, not anything about you. On iPhone and iPad, the app keeps its own saved progress, separate from Safari. To remove everything, delete the app from your home screen and clear the site's data.

2. Anonymous page-view counts

I use GoatCounter, a privacy-friendly counter, to see how many times each page is viewed. For each view it records:

  • the page (for example /physics-em/gauss) and the page or site that linked to it
  • general browser, operating system and screen-size information
  • an approximate country or region

GoatCounter does not use cookies and does not store your IP address. It does not build a profile of you or follow you to other websites. I can only see totals, such as "120 views of the Gauss's law page this week". On the course sites, each module you open counts as its own page view. On this landing page, opening a course card is counted the same way.

If your browser sends a Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signal, the sites don't load the counter at all. Ad blockers usually block it too, and the sites work the same either way.

3. Services that deliver the pages

Like any website, these pages are delivered by other companies. Your browser connects to them, which shares your IP address and basic browser details with them:

  • GitHub Pages hosts the sites. See the GitHub Privacy Statement.
  • Google Fonts provides the typefaces. See Google Fonts privacy.
  • GoatCounter, as described above.

4. Reporting an issue

"Report an issue" opens a pre-filled form on GitHub, in the public ap-labs-feedback repository. Nothing is sent until you press submit there. You need a GitHub account, and your report and username are public. Please don't include your real name, school, email or anything else personal. The pre-filled text only contains the course, the page and the site version.

5. Links to other sites

The sites link to other websites, such as College Board's AP Central, GitHub and LinkedIn. Those sites have their own privacy policies, which apply once you visit them.

6. Children and students

The sites are made for high school and college students studying for AP exams. They don't ask for, and are not designed to collect, personal information from anyone, including children under 13. If you think personal information was posted in a report, tell me and I'll remove it.

7. Your choices

  • Clear your browser's site data at any time to delete your saved progress.
  • Turn on Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control, or use a content blocker, to stop page-view counting.
  • Ask me to delete a report you filed, or anything else, using the contact options below.

8. Changes

If this policy changes, I'll update this page and the "Last updated" date at the top.

9. Contact

Open an issue in ap-labs-feedback, or send a private message on LinkedIn.
